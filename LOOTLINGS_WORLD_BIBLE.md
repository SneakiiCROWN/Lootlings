# Lootlings — Canonical World Bible

## Authority and delivery

The owner-approved Phase 2 master boards and canonical character sheets now govern beta creative direction. They may supply **deliberately cleaned, inspected individual beta exports**, not giant runtime sheets. The neutral Relic Beast remains the exact established mascot; the crystal-cat master-board concepts do not replace it. All names below are original Lootlings writing. Adult humanoid designs depict adults with jobs, agency and relationships, not creature costumes.

This document defines the story beneath the game, not additional combat mechanics. `world_story.gd` supplies short live lines. Field Notes exposes selected origins, residents and progressively revealed trails. Future cast/encounters described below are not claimed as implemented.

## The world, its wound and its treasure

The old civilisation of **Aurel Reach** stored promises in crystal: safe passage, a harvest, a home, a name remembered. These were Relic Cores, memory that could power useful things. A Core is not a soul or an interchangeable battery. Its glow records what it was asked to protect.

The Reach's rulers gathered those promises into the **First Hoard**, a vault intended to keep the world's shared obligations safe. The last Crown instead demanded ownership of every promise. During **Crownfall**, the vault broke, the sky-roads snapped and living fragments scattered into forests, furnaces and flooded workshops. Regions farther from ordinary settlements hold increasingly contradictory memories: frozen songs, rewritten stars, reflections of abandoned futures.

**Lootlings** woke around displaced Cores. No scholar agrees whether the fragments called them into being or found creatures already waiting. They are individuals, not elemental livestock. Their little bodies make ruins accessible; their alarming confidence makes oversized equipment seem reasonable. Treasure hunting brings back tools, medicine, records and occasional extremely bad ideas. Returning a relic to its purpose matters more than its price.

Ancient courier chests were keyed to promises and travelled along the Reach's roads. Crownfall scattered them. Some still protect their contents; some attract hungry things; some have learned to imitate hunger. A painted chest is not automatically a Mimic encounter—actual encounter rules must implement that risk before presenting it.

The **Hideout**, built around a surviving return-gate, is a refuge and working camp. Its lanterns, forge, shared meal barrel and Archive are practical institutions, not a royal court. Patch repairs tools, Ilex records recovered stories, Sera tends Cores and the Peddler brings news. Lootlings do not belong to any of them.

## Factions and the conflict

- **The Lantern Compact:** independent scavengers and merchants who honour safe routes and rescue debts. Patch and the Peddler disagree vigorously about what counts as a fair price.
- **The Quillkeepers:** relic scholars and record-keepers. Ilex preserves names; Sera preserves living purposes. Their disagreements are affectionate until someone suggests putting a price on a Core.
- **The Hollow Court:** surviving crown-servants who believe the Hoard still owns everything. Armoured collectors, vault apostles and infernal tribute-takers enforce a broken claim.
- **The Unwritten:** corrupted memories and people removed from the Reach's records. Not every strange creature is evil. A hungry reflection and a deliberately cruel ruler are different problems.

Demon corruption is a promise twisted into appetite: protection becomes possession, warmth becomes tribute, remembrance becomes erasure. Original demon designs are imposing, not recoloured playable Lootlings. Existing enemy IDs and attack contracts remain authoritative even when a beta sprite changes.

The First Hoard is the original shared trust, not simply a bigger pile of coins. The Hollow Crown is its surviving demand for ownership. A victory recovers one promise; it does not magically fix every wound. Future hooks: a Core carrying someone else's name, a sky-road leading beyond the map, and a courier chest delivered centuries late. These are story hooks, not shipped modes.

## The seven Lootlings

### Relic Beast — the neutral scavenger

Origin: found asleep in an old courier's satchel after Crownfall. Curious, loyal and distractible; fears leaving a friend behind. Likes odd keys, warm bread and well-packed bags; dislikes empty promises and treasure too large for its satchel. Hunts to return lost things, while occasionally confusing “lost” with “unclaimed.” Its Core brightens near return-gates. Counts snacks before checking weapons. Combat flavour: brave little steps behind ridiculous equipment; favourite gear is a patched satchel and whatever oversized tool works. Victory: delighted inventory inspection. Defeat: checks that companions got home, then blames the floor. Hideout: helps Patch sort Scrap into categories including “possibly edible.” Lines: “The chest sneezed. I sneezed back.” / “My satchel has room. Emotionally, at least.”

### Emberhorn — the rescue-furnace spark

Origin: woke in a cold rescue furnace, beside tools abandoned by fleeing workers. Warm-hearted, impatient and determined to be useful; fears letting others down, not ordinary flames. Likes repaired metal and applauding friends; dislikes waiting and waste. Hunts to keep the Hideout warm. Its Core remembers a promise of shelter, not a licence to burn everything. Proudly displays singed eyebrows as evidence of planning. Combat: emphatic weight-loading and brave follow-through; favours axes and furnace-forged pieces. Victory: loud celebration, then checks everybody's paws. Defeat: embarrassed, already proposing a better plan. Hideout: tends Patch's furnace. Lines: “Bravery first. Singed eyebrows later.” / “I warmed the treasure. You're welcome.”

### Mosskin — the watchstone gardener

Origin: grew around a watchstone protecting an abandoned garden. Patient, protective and quietly funny; fears arriving too late. Likes sturdy repairs and communal meals; dislikes careless trampling and possessive collectors. Hunts stolen relics for places that need them. Its Core remembers guardianship. Keeps a seed pouch labelled with names rather than species. Combat: planted stance and shield-first confidence; favours broad shields, heavy tools and moss-worn armour. Victory: checks on the smallest creature first. Defeat: accepts help without calling it surrender. Hideout: grows herbs behind the shrine. Lines: “Everyone behind me. Even the snacks.” / “That lock needs patience. Or a very large shield.”

### Moonkip — the bell-tower locksmith

Origin: appeared inside a locked bell tower before anyone found the key. Clever, proud and sneakily generous; fears being truly trapped. Likes impossible locks and silver buttons; dislikes noisy boasting by anyone else. Hunts keys and the secrets of sealed routes. Its Core remembers passage. Calls theft “temporary relocation” and always returns borrowed rescue gear. Combat: low springing posture and crossing slashes; favours crescent daggers and lightweight cloaks. Victory: pockets something insignificant as a souvenir. Defeat: insists the escape route was part of the plan. Hideout: tests locks, then tells Patch which failed. Lines: “I borrowed the key before we needed it.” / “Silent paws. Extremely loud treasure.”

### Pebblewyrm — the discarded foundation

Origin: hatched around a quarry's discarded foundation Core. Cheerful, stubborn and dependable; fears deep water. Likes well-made tools and carrying bags; dislikes being called disposable. Hunts pieces useful enough to build with. Its Core remembers supporting others. Names stones after people who helped move them. Combat: compact weight, blunt impact and patient recovery; favours mauls and tough partial armour. Victory: offers to carry the entire reward. Defeat: asks whether the bridge survived. Hideout: mends steps and moves furniture by accident. Lines: “I can carry it. I didn't ask how big.” / “The floor started it.”

### Glimmerfin — the drowned encore

Origin: woke when a song from the flooded Reach reached the surface. Theatrical and generous; fears silence that means nobody answered. Likes bells, reflections and listeners; dislikes performances interrupted by locked doors. Hunts relics that remember voices. Its Core stores the cadence of a return-song. Rehearses introductions while pretending to inspect loot. Combat: deliberate charge, expressive release and magical settle; favours staves, foci and tide-shaped jewellery. Victory: unsolicited encore. Defeat: bows, then asks for a second performance. Hideout: trades songs with Sera's humming crystals. Lines: “A dramatic entrance costs nothing.” / “The tide says shiny. I trust the tide.”

### Gloomlet — the unnoticed kindness

Origin: slipped out of a cracked shadow mirror. Dry, agile and privately affectionate; fears being forgotten. Likes unattended trinkets and warm corners; dislikes public thanks and accusatory lamps. Hunts things their owners stopped noticing. Its Core remembers recognition, not evil. Denies every helpful deed with suspicious precision. Combat: coiled posture, clawed snaps and sharp recovery; favours shadow-light daggers and small accessories. Victory: already beside the next exit. Defeat: returns quietly and makes sure the injured have blankets. Hideout: leaves found coins on Ilex's desk. Lines: “No witnesses. Except that judgmental lamp.” / “That coin was already following me.”

These are personality/pose directions, not new passives. All currently enabled identities retain equal power. Gloomlet still needs a clean runtime export; a damaged dark-fur extraction must not be presented as its canonical sprite.

## Art, loot and player-facing writing contracts

Life in the world; calm in the interface; chaos in the rewards. Gold/purple/cyan remains the UI material language. Common restrained metal, Rare blue, Epic purple, Legendary gold/orange; no new rarity probabilities. Cosmetic colour is not a status mechanic. Static flame/crystal ornament on beta weapons adds no Fire/Frost damage.

Existing jewellery, runes and named items deserve short lore, not new powers by implication. Suggested lore seeds: Relic Magnet remembers courier keys; Vault Receipt records a debt the Crown cannot collect; Echo Rune repeats an interrupted promise. No additional Signature effect is inferred from these stories.

Short lines belong in transitions and dialogue. Full origins belong in the optional Archive. Do not rename stable IDs because writing changes. Do not label an ordinary attack as a spell it does not perform. Missing bodies/outfits/VFX remain explicit production needs, not permanent programmer-art substitutes.

Simultaneous enemies, AoE, seeded dungeon routes, remaining Signatures and deeper evolution must follow a separately tested combat tranche. They are not implemented by this Bible.
