# Lootlings character voice and branding bible

Serious world. Ridiculous people. Real stakes. Cheeky reactions.

Mechanics precede flavour: an action, cost, consequence or clue gets a plain sentence. A second line can carry personality. Never obscure currency, equipment replacement, reward exclusivity, danger or unlock requirements behind a gag.

**Patch Brasswick:** adult travelling tinker; plain practical words, prices, wear, repairs and improbable warranties. A survivor who builds places to return to. He needles pretension, never customers for being poor. Warmth comes through what he does. Occasional adult innuendo can target his own disastrous dating or contracts, not cute creatures. Example: “That hinge survived the Crownfall. My last relationship managed three dinners.”

**Sera Vey:** adult relic scholar who rejected the Crown's commodification of living Cores. Calm, direct, emotionally attentive; practical instructions before ancient mysteries. Her humour punctures powerful people rather than frightened ones. Quiet grief and protection are allowed to land without a punchline. Example: “Ancient power can wait. You are bleeding on my notes.”

**Ilex Quill:** adult occult archivist, precise and sardonic; names, witnesses, annotations and evidence. Tyrants are bad editors. Her jokes have an exact target; historical erasure is serious. Example: “The Crown called it an authorised correction. The missing families disagreed.”

**Relic Beast:** curious loyal scavenger. Promises and returning lost things matter; snacks and bottomless-satchel confidence provide relief. **Emberhorn:** affectionate impatience, rescue-furnace warmth, safety plans written after ignition. **Mosskin:** patient protection and dry understatement. **Moonkip:** nimble, self-satisfied lock enthusiast; cannot leave a friend behind. **Pebblewyrm:** cheerful stubborn helper; literal jokes about load-bearing confidence. **Glimmerfin:** theatrical generosity, remembered voices and unrequested encores. **Gloomlet:** affectionate denial, stealth, dry observations; a shadow does not mean an erased identity. Keep creature voices distinct from adult innuendo.

**Enemies/bosses:** threatening verbs and concrete intent. Titles or short telegraphs communicate the next danger. Hoard Dragon treats stolen promises as inventory; Grump Knight serves an obsolete Crown and still expects payment. Their threat remains credible.

**Wanderers:** adult adventurers with established origins and dry/warm/bold/mystic voices. Broad genre awareness may inform a joke about dubious maps, sequels, dramatic entrances, tutorials or bureaucracy. No borrowed catchphrase, named franchise item, protected character impersonation or repeated single-franchise joke.

**Creative hierarchy:** Borderlands 2/3 informs reward exuberance and distinct voices; Diablo 1/2/3 informs danger, atmosphere and readable loot; Witcher 3 informs human consequences. Elder Scrolls leads fantasy/worldbuilding, with LOTR contributing old-world/mythic scale. Overwatch informs silhouette/readability and cosmetic appeal. Translate principles into original Lootlings writing and visuals.

**Branding:** “PACK LIGHT. COME HOME HEAVY.” is the canonical boot/title tagline. “RELICS. RUINS. RIDICULOUS WEAPONS.” is secondary promotional copy, not a rotating title tagline. Canonical gold/blue/purple remains; stronger ornament marks primary decisions and rare rewards, quieter surfaces mark utilities and filters.
